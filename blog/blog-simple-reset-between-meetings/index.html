A simple reset between meetings

A calendar can move quickly from one meeting to another. A short, intentional pause can help create a clear transition instead of carrying the momentum of one task directly into the next.

Create a transition

Before opening the next meeting, step away from the screen if possible. Sit or stand comfortably, relax the shoulders and take a moment to notice the breath. The goal is not to perform a complicated routine; it is to create a deliberate boundary between tasks.

Use the breath as an anchor

Bring attention to a comfortable, natural breath. Notice the movement of the abdomen or rib cage without forcing the inhale or exhale. If the mind is busy, simply return attention to the next breath. This can make the pause feel purposeful rather than like another interruption.

Move after long periods of sitting

A few gentle movements can change how the body feels after time at a desk. Roll the shoulders comfortably, move the neck within an easy range, stand up, walk briefly, or use simple Yoga movements appropriate to your ability. Avoid forcing stretches or moving into pain.

Close one task before opening another

A reset is also mental. Take one breath, identify the next priority and begin. This small ritual can help you shift attention instead of allowing unfinished thoughts from the previous meeting to dominate the next task.

Build a repeatable workplace habit

Organizations can make these transitions easier by creating space for wellbeing. A scheduled Yoga 1 Hour session gives teams a dedicated opportunity to move, breathe and prepare for work together, while individual resets can complement that broader routine.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers

Can I do a Yoga reset at work?

Yes. Simple, comfortable movements and mindful breathing can be practiced in an appropriate workplace space. Choose movements that suit your ability and environment.

How long should a reset take?

Even a few intentional minutes can create a transition, while a full Yoga 1 Hour session provides a more structured practice.

Is this a substitute for professional medical care?

No. General Yoga and breathing guidance is not a substitute for medical diagnosis or treatment. If you have a health concern, seek advice from an appropriately qualified professional.
